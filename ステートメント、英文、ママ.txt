<h2 class="H">Artist Statement</h2>

My work is related to one of the essential functions of photography: the inscription of one’s memory as a record. I deal with my subjects in relation to death, depicting, in some of my work, deceased relatives, the scenes of their funerals, the items they owned, and my dead pets. Photographing myself together with the images of those deceased and their possessions makes it in a way possible for me to coexist with them in images across time and space.

My work also reflects the notions I came to have from my life in Okinawa, where I grew up, and what my beloved mother, who passed away, often told me, about the issue of the female’s role in Okinawan society, where the patriarchal system still remains and where there are subtle cultural differences from the mainland of Japan. This seemingly oppressive culture against females and the history of the island drives me to find a way to liberate myself from oppression through my photographic production, which sometimes captures my own body, and the people and streets of Okinawa. 

The dreamy atmosphere of some of my photographs, heightened by their pale coloration, aims to express something fugacious which lies in between life and death. I also layer several pictures of scenes of Okinawa onto one image, creating an unrealistic landscape which does not indicate the real place. These visual alterations also help me seek for something constant even when my life, surrounding environment, and my work keep changing.

My photographs are printed in ink-jet and exhibited in various formats, from installations with framed photos in various sizes hung on the wall in white-cube spaces, to more intimate scales, like small printed photos in books and albums.
